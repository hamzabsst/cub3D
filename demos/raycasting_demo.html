🎯 Raycasting Explained Simply

What is Raycasting?

Imagine you're standing in a dark room with a flashlight. Raycasting is like shining that flashlight in different directions to see what walls are around you. That's exactly what games like Doom and Wolfenstein 3D do to create 3D-looking worlds!

🗺️ Step 1: The Map (Top-Down View)

What you see here:

  • 🟩 Green dot = You (the player)
  • ⬜ White squares = Walls
  • ⬛ Black squares = Empty space
  • 🔴 Red lines = Light rays you're "shooting"

🎯 Step 2: Shooting Rays

🔍 What Your Code Does (Step by Step)

1. raycast() function

Think of it as: "I want to look at the world through my eyes"

Your eyes don't see everything at once - they scan from left to right across your field of view. This function does exactly that: it shoots one ray for each pixel column on your screen (WIN_WIDTH rays total).

2. init_params() function

Think of it as: "Getting ready to throw a ball"

Before you throw a ball, you need to know: Where are you standing? Which direction will you throw? How hard? This function calculates all the math needed before shooting the ray.

3. perform_dda() function - The Magic!

Think of it as: "Walking in a straight line until you hit a wall"

Imagine you're walking through a grid city. At each intersection, you can go either right or forward. DDA (Digital Differential Analyzer) is a smart way to step through grid squares one by one until you hit a wall, without missing any squares.

4. set_texture() function

Think of it as: "What kind of wall did I hit?"

When you hit a wall, you need to know: Is it a north wall? South wall? East wall? West wall? Each wall type might have different textures (like brick, wood, etc.)

🧠 The Big Picture

Your code is like having super-vision that can instantly:

  1. Look in hundreds of directions at once
  2. Measure exactly how far away each wall is
  3. Figure out what type of wall it hit
  4. Use all this info to draw a 3D-looking scene on a 2D screen

The brilliant part? It's all done with simple 2D math, but creates the illusion of 3D!
