🎨 Wall Drawing & Texture Mapping

🎯 What This Code Does

After raycasting finds a wall, this code:

  • Calculates how tall the wall should appear on screen
  • Maps a texture image onto that wall slice
  • Draws one vertical column of pixels to the screen

This happens for every column of your screen (800+ times per frame!)

📐 Step-by-Step Breakdown

Step 1: Calculate Wall Height

wall_height = (int)(WIN_HEIGHT / hit.distance);

The Magic Formula! The farther the wall, the smaller it appears.

Distance = 2.0 → Wall Height = 200 pixels

Step 2: Center the Wall on Screen

wall_top = (WIN_HEIGHT - wall_height) / 2;
wall_bottom = wall_top + wall_height;

Centers the wall vertically. Remaining space becomes sky/floor.

Wall Top = 100, Wall Bottom = 300

Step 3: Find Texture Column

tex_x = (int)(hit.hit_point * texture_width) % texture_width;

Critical! Which column of the texture image to use?

Hit Point = 0.30 → Texture X = 19

Step 4: Stretch Texture Vertically

tex_y = (y - wall_top) * texture_height / wall_height;

Maps each screen pixel to the correct texture pixel.

As screen Y goes from 100 to 300, texture Y goes from 0 to 63.

🎨 The Texture Mapping Process

Look at the visualization above:

  1. Left: The original texture (64x64 brick pattern)
  2. Middle: One column extracted from the texture (the green line)
  3. Right: That column stretched to fit the wall height on screen
// This is the pixel copying magic:
color = *(int *)(texture_address + texture_pixel_position);
*(int *)(screen_address + screen_pixel_position) = color;

Each pixel gets copied from texture → screen buffer, creating the illusion of a textured 3D wall!

🚀 Why This Creates 3D

  • Distance Scaling: Far walls = short columns, Near walls = tall columns
  • Texture Mapping: Makes flat surfaces look detailed and realistic
  • Perspective: The human eye interprets different column heights as depth

The result: Your brain sees a 3D corridor, but it's really just hundreds of stretched texture columns!
